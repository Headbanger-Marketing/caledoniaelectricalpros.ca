EV charger installation in Caledonia.

Charging a vehicle at a Caledonia home changes how the parking space is used and raises the question of what is already wired. An outlet or builder rough-in is a starting point, not proof it can be reused.

The charger is only one part of the installation

An EV charging project includes equipment, a suitable connection and an assessment of the property supply. ESA advises using approved charging equipment, a dedicated circuit and outdoor-rated equipment where needed. The contractor should evaluate the proposed load and explain whether the supply, a load-management arrangement or another design is suitable. Do not choose a circuit from the charger advertising alone.

Prepare the scope for your Caledonia property

Photograph any outlet in the garage or near the driveway that looks meant for a charger, and write down where it sits relative to the parking spot and the panel. Collect the charger instructions if a model is chosen.

Ask the contractor what evidence shows an existing outlet or builder rough-in is suitable, and whether a new dedicated circuit would be proposed instead. Ask what the written scope says about the route to the garage.

What should the assessment establish?

A charging spot is coming to a Caledonia garage, and the scope has to let the homeowner choose whether to go ahead and when. It records whether the existing outlet or rough-in is suitable or due for replacement, weighing any garage circuits shared with rooms in the house and the other loads on the supply. Approved charging equipment, the dedicated circuit and outdoor-rated equipment where the location needs it are named. Whether a capacity assessment supports the proposed load is stated as well. The scope confirms that the contractor handles the ESA notification before charging begins.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance, and EV charging safety guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can the outlet the builder left in my garage charge a vehicle?

Possibly, but the outlet's presence does not prove it is suitable. The contractor should assess the circuit behind it, the equipment you plan to use and the condition of the connection before reusing anything.

Is a rough-in in the wall the same as a finished charger circuit?

No. A rough-in is preparation only, and it may stop short of the panel or lack a suitable connection. Ask for a written statement of what is already in place and what still has to be added.
