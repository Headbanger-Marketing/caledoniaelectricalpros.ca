Panel and service upgrades in Caledonia.

A new nursery, gym or workshop in a Caledonia house can fill the last spare breaker spaces. Whether the answer is more positions or a larger supply is a separate question for a contractor to assess.

A panel and an electrical service are different parts of the system

The panel distributes electricity to property circuits. The service is the incoming supply and associated equipment. Replacing a panel does not automatically increase the available supply. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess existing condition and demand, planned loads and the utility requirements before recommending equipment. Spare breaker spaces alone do not establish capacity.

Prepare the scope for your Caledonia property

List the equipment each changed room will add, such as a space heater, a treadmill or a desktop setup, and take a photo of the labels on the panel door without opening it.

Ask the contractor whether the plan adds circuit positions, raises the supply, or both, and whether a panel in the garage would be fed from the main one. Ask which new loads the recommendation assumes.

What should the assessment establish?

When a room in a Caledonia house changes use, spare positions, available supply or neither may run short, and the contractor's finding names which. The report lists the existing loads, the new equipment and the devices likely to run at the same time. If a garage panel is proposed, the scope describes how it is fed and what it serves. Anything left unseen because a cover stayed closed is named as an assumption. As for the utility, the written proposal states whether it must be involved and what disconnection the work requires.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Will a larger panel let the house power a new room's equipment?

Not by itself. A larger panel can add places for breakers, while the supply entering the house stays as it is unless the service itself is changed. Ask the contractor to explain which problem your new room use actually creates.

Can a garage subpanel be added for a workshop or gym?

It may be possible, but it depends on the route from the main panel, the planned equipment and the existing supply. A Licensed Electrical Contractor must assess it and file the ESA notification for the work.
